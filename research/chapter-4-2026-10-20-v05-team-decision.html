4.1 Scope and principal finding

This chapter reports the completed synthetic-data experiments for the AICS case reference prototype. The fixed main test failed. On the 36 non-transport reviewed cases at p = .50 and five retrieved neighbors, equal-weight retrieval had mean absolute error (MAE) of ₱1,903.82, compared with ₱1,333.76 for the same-purpose baseline. The resulting reduction was -42.74%, rather than the required reduction of at least 20%. The maximum MAE compatible with that criterion was ₱1,067.01. These results do not establish that the proposed retrieval method works on this dataset under the study's stated success criterion.

In the historical pre-correction run, all four non-medical assistance types show steadily increasing reductions in case-base retrieval error relative to their condition-specific purpose baselines as planted household signal increases; this ordered pattern is descriptive and does not change the fixed reviewed-case failure.

The historical type-pool weight-learning experiment improved over equal weights under its specified tests. That finding addresses a different comparison and does not replace the failed main test. In particular, improvement also appeared in the no-household-signal control. It cannot, by itself, establish recovery of household structure or usefulness in real benefit decisions.

All records and evaluated grants are synthetic. No social worker answered the case sheets or used the prototype in this study. There was no user acceptance testing (UAT), System Usability Scale (SUS) exercise, or collection of social-worker judgments. “Reviewed cases” below names the supplied forty-case packet and does not denote human outcome evaluation. The current Chapters 1–3 source is Manuscript v27 dated 20 October 2026.

The pool amendment and the missing-value correction were decided after the fixed main test had been run and had failed. Both the original type-pool result and the amended purpose-pool result are reported, and the original result is not withdrawn.

The corrected type-pool and purpose-pool fixed tests also fail, at −49.05% and −5.13% reduction respectively. Separately, on the case base, purpose pooling changes medical’s equal-weight reduction from −337.07% to +10.26% at p = .50 and produces +22.40% at p75. This is an important diagnostic improvement, not a replacement for the failed fixed test.

The research team decided to make the two amendments on 20 October 2026 without first seeking Ma’am Grace’s agreement. No adviser approval or prospective registration of the amendments is claimed. The settings for the new comparisons were recorded before generating corrected outcomes. Sections 4.2–4.10 retain the historical pre-correction study, including its failed results and diagnostic analyses; Sections 4.13–4.17 report the new follow-ups. This chapter ends at Chapter 4.

4.2 Historical implementation and provenance

The comparison uses the 39 fields specified in the manuscript, same-type reference pools, applicant-level exclusion, reference-only numeric ranges and constants, Jaccard scores for multi-select fields, and the specified treatment of unknowns, not-applicable values and shared absences. All events of a held-out applicant are excluded. The reviewed cases are never inserted into the case base; the evaluator also applies their documented partner exclusions. The prototype displays five past cases using equal weights and does not output a prediction or applicant score.

The reconciled implementation restores the supplied amount-code scoring semantics and sorted-payload noise algorithm. Compatibility tests reproduce all 160 supplied forty-case amounts with the supplied calibration. The study run instead uses the required calibration from the 1,000-event case base and reuses it for the forty test cases. Per-event scoring avoids the source script's repeated-applicant overwrite. Cost and rounding constraints are enforced explicitly.

Researcher-selected cost ranges were retained from the earlier preparation, not changed in response to its failed result. They use uniform intervals from 0.5 to 1.5 times available cost benchmarks; device and transport ranges remain declared assumptions. Educational costs use a tuition proxy. Laboratory uses the published ratio .9052; dialysis and chemotherapy each use .3556 from their combined source row. These completions and proxies must remain disclosed in the methodology.

The existing base had 244 wage-label contradictions. The reconciled copy removes the contradictory labels without changing incomes or worker counts. New generated sets enforce that consistency before inserting unknown values. Ten original-base events remain unverifiable because worker counts are missing; they are flagged, not presented as validated. Unknown values are still excluded from Gower. The source amount rule's distinct missing-score semantics are preserved and are a limitation of the synthetic mechanism.

The earlier provisional run is retained separately: its main-test retrieval MAE was ₱1,930.18 against ₱923.49, a −109.01% reduction. The reconciled run is a distinct version justified by source compatibility, not a replacement advertised as success.

The purpose-baseline MAE also rose from ₱923.49 in the provisional run to ₱1,333.76 in the reconciled run (44.43%); reconciliation changed the generated outcomes and calibration used by the comparator, so this is a change of run definition, not an improvement measured against a fixed baseline.

4.7 Historical five-fold learned weights and thirty-set evidence

Applicant IDs were assigned to five fixed folds. Each fold's shared 39 weights were fitted only on the other four folds, using applicant leave-one-out retrieval inside that training pool and 200 objective evaluations. All weights were nonnegative and summed to 39. Held-out applicants retrieved from all other case-base applicants, including other applicants assigned to their test fold; the equal-weight comparator used that identical reference pool. Settings selected on the separate set were held fixed. No reviewed case selected weights or budget.

Gain across household-signal levels

The thirty-set scaled gains are .023033, .022640, .023645 and .025527 at p = 0, .25, .50 and .75. Their spread is 0.002887, smaller than the mean replicate SD of 0.005854. The gain is descriptively flat rather than showing a clear increase with household signal. This range-to-SD comparison is a descriptive diagnostic, not a formal trend or equivalence test. Together with improvement at p = 0 and the weight-mass results below, it identifies purpose recovery without detectable household-pattern recovery at any tested signal level under this implementation and evidence. It does not prove that no household information could be recovered by any method.

4.12 Interpretation and limitations

The fixed reviewed-case test does not support the proposed retrieval method's success on this generated data under its fixed peso-error criterion. Separately, learned weights improve over equal weights within the implemented synthetic experiments. The control result and the large role of purpose limit an interpretation based on household-pattern recovery. Statistical improvement in scaled error is also distinct from passing the pooled peso test.

The data-generating mechanism includes assumed costs, a flattened type mix, chosen missingness, historical income thresholds and a known amount rule. Its source scoring treats some unknown inputs as absence or zero, while retrieval does not impute them. These choices restrict external validity. The forty-case test has small type-specific counts, so those rows should not be treated as precise type-level estimates. The fixed greedy search budget is reproducible but does not guarantee globally best weights. No result here estimates the accuracy, fairness or operational usefulness of decisions on actual DSWD applicants.

The complete evidence retains all seeds, fold assignments, event predictions, applicant errors, comparison counts, candidate traces and source hashes. Failed results and unverified external feedback remain visible. The chapter is ready for the research team's substantive review, not presented as an approved thesis chapter or evidence of successful real-world deployment.

A possible interpretation is a trade-off between household matching and the variability of a five-neighbor median. The purpose baseline uses all eligible same-purpose reference events, while retrieval uses five. For cost-bearing cases both combine grant-to-cost shares; for food and cash relief they combine amounts directly. The baseline estimates a purpose-level median, whereas retrieval tries to exploit household-specific information. They need not estimate the same conditional quantity when household effects exist. Retrieval benefits only if the useful local information outweighs the error associated with its smaller selected set. The observed k pattern is consistent with a benefit from averaging over more neighbors, but it does not isolate estimator variance from selection bias, distance quality, pool composition or other causes. No variance decomposition was performed. Likewise, the non-monotonic forty-case reductions (+2.94%, −29.51%, −5.13%, +3.41%) cannot simply be declared noise from their size alone.

The main setting stays k = 5 and p = .50. Neither the better k = 10 sensitivity value nor the stronger p75 results were substituted for the fixed test. Studying signal strength in independently obtained real records, per-type weights on a larger base, and the neighbor-count trade-off are future research questions for Chapter 5; no new experiment or operational commitment follows from this discussion.
